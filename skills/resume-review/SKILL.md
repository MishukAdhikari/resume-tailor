# Reviewing a resume

Use a skim, scan, and study sequence as review exercises. The time budgets are
heuristics, not universal measurements of recruiter behavior. Complete all passes and
always run [resume-evidence-gate](../resume-evidence-gate/SKILL.md), even if the layout fails.

Use the target posting if supplied. Otherwise label relevance judgments as provisional.
If only text is available, review the wording and mark visual layout and file parsing as
not checked. Do not claim to have inspected a PDF or DOCX you have not opened.

| Pass | Approximate exercise budget | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skim** | 2 seconds | Titles, keywords, and the most visible information |
| **Scan** | 10 seconds | First bullets, relevance, and page structure |
| **Study** | 1 minute or more | Evidence, contribution, context, and consistency |

## Pass 1: Skim

- [ ] Do titles and prominent keywords communicate the match to the role?
- [ ] Are employment titles accurate? If a functional descriptor is needed, is it clearly
      separate from the official title rather than a replacement or upgrade?
- [ ] Are the posting's supported capabilities and explicitly requested tools easy to find?
- [ ] Does page one carry the strongest relevant evidence?

## Pass 2: Scan

Read the first bullet of each role and inspect the page structure.

- [ ] Does each first bullet lead with a supported result or concrete contribution, with
      a metric only when available and verified?
- [ ] Is it the strongest relevant bullet, rather than simply the largest number?
- [ ] Are headings clear, dates consistent, and bullets concise enough to scan?
- [ ] Does the submitted file use selectable text and a simple reading order? Prefer one
      column; keep essential information out of images, text boxes, headers, and footers.
- [ ] Does extracted or copied text preserve names, contact details, dates, titles, and
      bullet order? When the application shows parsed fields, check those too.

Follow the employer's file-type instructions. Inspect the rendered export as well as its
text. These checks reduce parsing problems; they cannot certify every ATS configuration.

## Pass 3: Study

### Curate the experience

- [ ] Do recent and relevant roles get most of the space? Use the last five to seven years
      as a starting point, retaining older evidence when it matters to this application.
- [ ] Are older roles reduced to two or three relevant bullets, a compact entry, or omitted
      when appropriate? Avoid creating a misleading employment timeline.
- [ ] Does each detail help explain the candidate's fit?

### Preserve a specific, natural voice

- [ ] Replace abstractions such as "intelligent systems" with the actual work or system.
- [ ] Edit repetitive openings and long clauses where it improves readability, without
      changing ownership or forcing artificial sentence-length variation.
- [ ] Preserve useful, sourced details such as "4.6 MB app budget", "seven cloud providers",
      and "proven on a real device". These are examples, not facts about this candidate.
- [ ] Check precise details at source. Neither specificity nor writing style proves human
      authorship, and AI can invent convincing numbers.

Editing tools may help with grammar and clarity. Compare rewrites against the source so
that facts, scope, and the candidate's voice survive the edit.

### Explain transitions where useful

- [ ] Would a gap, short stint, or pivot benefit from a brief factual explanation?
- [ ] Has the candidate confirmed both the explanation and what they want to disclose?
- [ ] Do titles and dates agree with the supplied records and profiles?

Offer a neutral one-line explanation where useful. "Role ended in a company
restructuring" or "Personal sabbatical, returning full-time" may fit only
if confirmed. Never invent a reason or assume current availability. Do not require private
health or family details; the candidate may choose a neutral "Career break" or no explanation.

### Build the bridge and balance strengths

- [ ] Does each major achievement connect to a stated business need in familiar language?
- [ ] Across the document, is there relevant evidence of technical or domain competence,
      business impact, and leadership or collaboration?
- [ ] Are soft skills demonstrated through actions such as mentoring, coordinating a
      handoff, or resolving a customer issue?

Balance these strengths to suit the role and seniority. Do not force all three into every
bullet or invent management responsibility for an individual contributor.

### Check honesty regardless of earlier failures

- [ ] Every claim has an evidence status under the evidence gate; unresolved facts are
      withheld from final copy and listed as questions.
- [ ] Numbers have a source and meaningful outcome or scale context.
- [ ] Ownership verbs match the candidate's actual contribution.
- [ ] Qualitative outcomes are supported too; removing a number does not verify a result.

## Application readiness

When reviewing a specific application, also check:

- Required questions and attachments are complete, and answers agree with the resume.
- Optional questions remain the candidate's choice; missing facts are not guessed.
- The deadline, resume version, and posting link are recorded if provided. Prepare promptly
  when practical, without sacrificing accuracy merely to apply early.
- Suggest a simple tracker for company, role, link, deadline, version, status, and next step
  when the user needs help managing several applications.

Reviewing does not itself authorize submitting an application or contacting anyone.

## The verdict

Return an overall readiness assessment, prioritized findings with locations and suggested
fixes, unresolved evidence questions, and coverage limits. Put unsupported claims first,
then relevance and layout, then wording. Summarize all three passes even when one fails.
If edits are requested, apply supported fixes and identify any missing input that prevents
completion. Do not infer the cause of rejection from the resume alone.
